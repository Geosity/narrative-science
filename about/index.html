About Narrative Science

The proposed journal investigates narrative both as an object of scientific study and as a method used in explanation, modeling, learning, and decision making.

How we explain science.
How we imagine what comes next.

Exploring narrative in scientific inquiry, computational models, and scientific explanation.

Our Perspective

Scientific narratives connect a research question to the models, observations, and reasoning used to address it. In engineering, they can explain how a system behaves, why a design decision was made, and how evidence supports its performance. We are interested in accounts that make these connections explicit and open to examination.

Focus and Scope

We aim to connect conceptual inquiry with computational and empirical research. Contributions should explain what they mean by narrative, how it informs their methods, and what evidence supports their conclusions.

Core Topics

  • Narrative explanation and causal reasoning in science and engineering.
  • Scientific modeling, simulation, and experimental evidence.
  • Knowledge representation, machine reasoning, and explainable intelligent systems.
  • Parallel intelligence, multi-agent systems, and cyber-physical-social systems.
  • Intelligent control, autonomous systems, and engineering decision support.
  • Robotics, embodied intelligence, and human–machine collaboration.
  • Digital twins and model-based systems engineering.
  • Intelligent manufacturing, industrial automation, and fault diagnosis.
  • Intelligent transportation, energy systems, and sustainable infrastructure.
  • Uncertainty, safety, and responsible communication in scientific and engineering practice.

Article Types

Original research, reviews and tutorials, methodological papers, perspectives and debates, and evidence-based case studies.

Editorial Principles

Claims should be proportionate to evidence. Conceptual research should make its arguments explicit; computational and empirical work should document methods and limitations.
